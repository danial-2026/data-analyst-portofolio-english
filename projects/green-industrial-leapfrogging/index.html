What the spatial statistic says

Positive clustering. Countries with similar Y values tend to be near one another more often than expected under spatial randomness, conditional on the supplied weights.
  • The series spans 97 countries each year, with five neighbours per country in the reported KNN setup.

  • Spatial autocorrelation motivates spatial models; it does not identify the mechanism behind the clustering.

06 / Methodology and limits

Evidence notes and reproducibility

Research design

  • Panel: 97 Asian and African countries, 2010–2024, 1,455 country-year observations.
  • Outcome Y is a composite Green Industrial Leapfrogging Index.
  • Core explanatory dimensions: X1 AI-enabling digital capability; X2 digital transformation; X3 sustainable innovation; plus institutional quality.
  • Methods include panel regression, spatial diagnostics / Spatial Durbin Model, machine-learning comparison, KernelSHAP, and Monte Carlo predictive scenarios.

Important data-quality and interpretation caveats

  • Model performance mismatch: the paper abstract reports LightGBM test R² = 0.685, but the supplied model-comparison CSV reports LightGBM test R² = 0.371 and XGBoost test R² = 0.405. This dashboard uses the supplied CSV and flags the discrepancy for reconciliation.
  • Scenario model: Monte Carlo summaries are predictive scenarios, not causal effects. All supplied intervals cross zero.
  • Spatial impacts: interpret direct / indirect effects within the supplied model and weights matrix. Estimates do not prove policy causation.
  • Leapfrogging shares: Asia 40.2%, Africa 37.0% are reproduced from the paper abstract; verify the operational definition before publication.
  • Public data: the page embeds aggregate statistics and country-year Y series to support interactive plots, not raw text records. Review dataset licensing and confidentiality before public deployment.
