Network Contagion Index (NCI)

What the index means

NCI is a composite, study-specific score combining centrality and selected edge-related signals. Higher values indicate a more structurally connected position according to the chosen weights—not a calibrated likelihood of contagion.

The count is the number of documents in which both dimensions co-occurred. Association strength is a separate normalized measure from the supplied network output. A large count and a high association strength answer different questions.

05 / Research transparency

Methodology & limitations

The dashboard is designed to make the analytical logic inspectable, not to overstate what media-derived indicators can prove.

Network construction

The supplied manifest describes a document-level co-occurrence network with six dimensions and nine observed edges. Two dimensions are connected when their signals appear in the same document under the study's mapping procedure.

Edge count, co-occurrence rate, and association strength are different measures and should not be treated as interchangeable.

NCI construction

The manifest reports the weights: centrality 0.30, crisis-edge signal 0.30, trust-edge signal 0.20, and betweenness 0.20. The displayed NCI values are the supplied computed results.

These are model-dependent structural scores. They are not probabilities, official ratings, or validated measures of actual institutional contagion.

Temporal signals & EWS

The monthly file contains 33 observations. The manifest identifies Isolation Forest for anomaly detection and the formula EWS = 0.45 × SEI reference + 0.35 × NCI + 0.20 × anomaly score. Yellow and red thresholds are reported as the 75th and 90th quantiles.

Unusual observations may reflect true changes, small monthly sample sizes, data-collection variation, or model behavior. They require contextual review.

Integrated GCR proxy

The manifest gives GCR = 0.55 × GCI reference + 0.30 × NCI + 0.15 × anomaly score. This is an analytical proxy for governance-contagion exposure, not an official BPKH risk rating and not causal evidence.

Key limitations

  • Media visibility is not the same as underlying risk incidence.
  • Co-occurrence does not establish causality or the direction of influence.
  • Automated labels and semantic scores can contain classification error.
  • Thresholds and weights affect the resulting signals.
  • Low document counts in a month can make normalized indicators volatile.
  • Governance Oversight appears isolated in the supplied network; this means no qualifying edges were observed under this specification, not that oversight is absent in reality.

Responsible interpretation

Use the outputs as a research-based monitoring framework to prioritize questions for further review. Do not use the scores alone to accuse individuals, infer misconduct, or make high-stakes decisions. Any operational deployment would require validation, documented data governance, human review, and independent assessment.
